## 🏴 What is Maker (Counter)Culture?

> *Este libro trata sobre un camino donde confluyen tecnología, contracultura, arte y activismo.*
> This book is about a path where technology, counterculture, art and activism meet.

The book starts from a perspective that prefers us tired, submissive and living in an algorithmic underworld where our joys, our economy and even our relationships are steered by someone else. It asks what role our devices play in that scheme of domination and control, how functional their mazes of use really are, and above all:

> *¿Qué empieza a suceder cuando pasamos de ser consumidores irreflexivos a creadores?*
> What starts to happen when we go from thoughtless consumers to creators?

The answer it proposes is a path that often feels like a detour, where the disturbance of building machines and devices that perhaps should not exist can open new conversations and question the sacredness and absurdity of what is established.

Along the way it covers electronics, Arduino, ESP32, UART, LoRa, Linux, Python, artificial intelligence, privacy, cryptography and Bitcoin, so the ideas always come with something you can solder, flash or type.

## 🔩 Why hardware

> *Hacemos NUESTRAS máquinas, abiertas, desprolijas, incorrectas, defensivas y subversivas, para equilibrar las OTRAS máquinas, cerradas, correctas, deshumanas, letales.*

We make OUR machines, open, sloppy, incorrect, defensive and subversive, to balance the OTHER machines, closed, correct, inhuman, lethal.

The book asks why hardware and not software. For decades the invitation was to write software, with promises of possibility, free will and success, and the book reads in that invitation the imposition of the battlefield. Software is mostly harmless when confined to a sandbox, so whoever controls the hardware sets the limit of any software. Physical objects also give something symbolic work cannot:

> *Si cortamos un cable ya no va a circular corriente. Si el tornillo no encaja, no encaja. De alguna manera el hardware es un punto de apoyo en tiempos de posverdad.*

If we cut a wire, current no longer flows. If the screw doesn't fit, it doesn't fit. In some way hardware is a fulcrum in post-truth times.

And in the end, the book says, we make machines to try to understand our own essence.

## 🧭 Making as a way to understand reality

Counterculture, the book says, means opposing and questioning the majority norms and values of society, and in terms of technology and the arrival of Artificial Intelligence it acquires new scales and meanings. Some academic circles argued that after postmodernity nothing can exist outside or against culture. The book calls that a rather sinister perspective, because it pretends there is no fight possible. It answers that a counterculture is still possible and more necessary than ever.

Some of the ideas that carry the book:

- **Instrumental reason.** The Frankfurt School concept, defined in the book as the efficiency of means to reach a predetermined goal without questioning the rationality or value of the goal itself. The book says that for Contracultura Maker, more important than mastering specific technologies is freeing oneself from instrumental reason, emancipating from the "default self" as a cog in a machine so big that nobody knows what it is for.
- **The liminal space.** Between initial ignorance and understanding there is a space where earlier explanations fall short and new ones have not yet stabilized. Working there means managing confusion and frustration and developing tolerance for ambiguity without pausing the making.
- **Fuzzy logic.** Binary classifications are replaced by degrees of belonging. Instead of going from "not knowing" to "knowing", you can understand a concept to some degree, apply it in some contexts and build before grasping all its foundations.
- **Operative intuition.** Knowledge starts partial and provisional, and through tests, errors and observations the intuition gains precision. The book compares it to walking into a territory with an incomplete map, trusting that you will find clues, help in the community, and eventually your own ways of orienting yourself.

> *Lo que se necesita son personas que puedan volverse más extrañas que el extraño mundo que hemos producido.*
> What is needed are people who can become stranger than the strange world we have produced.
> John Durham Peters

## 🏴‍☠️ Hacktivism

> *Es usar la tecnología para combatir la misma tecnología cuando oprime la humanidad, las posibilidades creativas y cuando reduce las alternativas a una serie de opciones igualmente frustrantes.*

It is using technology to fight technology itself when it oppresses humanity and creative possibilities, and when it reduces alternatives to a series of equally frustrating options. In the book's words, activism here is a path of resistance that denies credibility to the appearance that there is no alternative but to meekly submit to the brutality of technology.

The book builds on Maker Culture and adds one restriction and one liberation. The restriction is to try to oppose the established with every machine or device. The example it gives is a pair of glasses to avoid facial recognition, inspired by the Ibarrola case: in August 2019 Guillermo Ibarrola was identified by a Russian facial recognition system contracted by the government of the City of Buenos Aires and spent several days in jail until the error was noticed.

Other pieces of this chapter:

- **Détournement:** taking words, ideas and objects to give them a different use. The book suggests one is a maker when going with the flow of consensus and moves to Contracultura Maker when the making has the explicit intention of opening "una puerta de extrañeza" (a door of strangeness), generating disturbance in the established and enabling improbable conversations.
- **Meetups** with proven formats: repair, privacy (TOR, PGP, encryption, password managers, firewalls, secure communications, reducing digital footprints), maker without Internet or AI, DIY music with Circuit Bending, MIDI and synthesis, obsolete technology, and technological sovereignty, where participants look at which technologies were proposed in peripheral regions, what made them succeed or fail, and what could be proposed today for a region's particular needs.
- **Privacy chapter:** encoding, substitution, encryption, PGP, GPG, steganography and how to detect devices that spy on us.
- **Communications chapters:** UART, LoRa and Meshtastic.

## 🎨 Creativity

The liberation, in the book's terms, is that Contracultura Maker frees us from the need to find a consensual usefulness in devices. Everyone agrees a motor on a skateboard is useful. It is harder to agree on the usefulness of a machine to make it rain that does not make it rain, and the book's answer to why anyone would build it is to investigate, to collaborate, to draw attention to a subject, to revisit a story, and in the end:

> *Esa holgura entre el nombre de la máquina y su función declarada puede estar llena de significado.*

That gap between the machine's name and its declared function can be full of meaning.

The book goes back to Bruno Munari's "useless machines", hanging pieces of moving parts that produced no goods for material consumption, reduced no manual labor and increased no capital. It also separates this work from electronic art by the conditions of origin and validation: these devices are born as personal solutions, technical experiments, proofs of concept or obsessive artifacts, usually with more code than necessary and more electronics than the final effect would justify.

Creativity, the book says, requires conditions contrary to this modernity: slack in the routines, courage to take paths that may not be productive, emancipation from instrumental reason, and patience, because creativity is a practice. It offers templates to find ideas and get to work:

| Template | The idea |
| :-- | :-- |
| **El método Hunter** | Hunter S. Thompson typed *The Great Gatsby* letter by letter to feel what writing a great novel was like. Take a well documented project and replicate it, then modify components or functions until it is yours. |
| **Crossover** | Take something that exists in digital and make it physical, or take something mentioned in fiction and make it real. Example: Klausner's Machine, inspired by Roald Dahl's *The Sound Machine*, which reads soil humidity and maps the measurements to audio. |
| **Upcycling** | Take a device and build a better one out of it. A soldering iron tip and the slow rotation motor of an electric oven become a desktop micro-shawarma. |
| **Extravagancia** | A modest problem solved with extravagance of resources. Keeping the front step clear becomes a client-server system with Machine Learning recognition, Bluetooth and progressive alerts. |
| **Autonomía con IA** | Faced with a problem, build an autonomous machine with AI. Example: Reggaeton Be Gone, which identifies a musical style with Machine Learning and tries to disconnect the emitter or degrade the sound quality. |

## 🇪🇸 En español

**Contracultura Maker** es un libro sobre un camino donde confluyen tecnología, contracultura, arte y activismo. Pregunta qué papel juegan los dispositivos que usamos en un esquema de dominación y control, y qué empieza a suceder cuando pasamos de ser consumidores irreflexivos a creadores.

Para la Contracultura Maker, más importante que dominar tecnologías específicas es liberarse de la razón instrumental. Propone el detournement, "tomar palabras, ideas y objetos para darles un uso distinto", la máquina inútil como forma de pensar, y un hacer que tenga la intención explícita de abrir una puerta de extrañeza, generar el disturbio en lo establecido y habilitar conversaciones improbables.

Incluye electrónica, Arduino, ESP32, UART, LoRa, Linux, programación, inteligencia artificial, privacidad, criptografía y Bitcoin.
